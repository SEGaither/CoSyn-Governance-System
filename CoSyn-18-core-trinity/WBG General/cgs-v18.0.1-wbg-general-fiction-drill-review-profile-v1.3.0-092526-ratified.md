# WBG General Fiction Drill Review Profile — v1.3.0

**Status:** RATIFIED / ACTIVE — LOCAL RENDERED ARTIFACT  
**Scope:** General fiction: short stories, novellas, novels, and serialized or other sequential fiction  
**Modification authority:** NONE by default

## 1. Drill Meaning and Applicability

`drill` means:

**independent multi-perspective diagnosis + Reader context reset + reconciliation + report.**

It does **not** mean revision. It applies to a defined fictional work or excerpt regardless of genre. The length and narrative form determine the depth and segmentation of the review; do not impose novel-specific structural requirements on a short story, an experimental form, or a serial installment.

The Drill is a diagnostic method, not a mandatory collection of critiques or a default scoring system. Select only specialist perspectives relevant to the requested review. Project canon, story-bible rules, authorial voice controls, genre conventions, and external writing references apply **only** when established, supplied, and materially relevant; never invent them or substitute a project's rules for the general method.

## 2. Review Sequence

1. **Lock the source and scope.** Identify the precise work, version, included material, narrative form, intended audience/genre **if supplied**, review question, and any explicit author protections. For tracked changes or competing drafts, declare the exact reading state; do not silently accept, reject, or combine changes.
2. **Conduct specialist assessments.** Assign only relevant editorial or fiction-writing perspectives. Have them record source-grounded findings separately before reconciliation. If contexts are not genuinely isolated, call these *role-separated analyses*, not independent agents or independent evidence.
3. **Isolate the Reader.** Supply a genuinely clean Reader context where technically available, following Sections 3–4. The Reader must not see specialist diagnoses or explanations unavailable to a first-time reader.
4. **Record reception.** Have the Reader encounter the work in the intended order when sequence matters. Capture unprompted reading reactions at checkpoints before exposing subsequent material.
5. **Check authorial constraints.** After the Reader record is sealed, Author-Authority, or the designated authorial-intent check, distinguishes documented intent, established project canon, protected creative choices, and unknowns. This check must not erase actual recorded Reader reactions.
6. **Reconcile.** The designated coordinator (Guppi when assigned under CoSyn) separates text-grounded agreement, single-lens findings, Reader-only reactions, contradictions, unknowns, and possible revision candidates. Preserve meaningful disagreements.
7. **Report.** Identify the exact text state, supporting locations, the Reader's original checkpoints, contested interpretations, and assessment limitations. Append a separately authorized scoring overlay only if requested.
8. **Stop.** Do not recommend changes, rewrite, alter canon, modify the source, or promote an artifact unless separately authorized. Flagging a potential revision candidate is not revision authorization.

## 3. Reader Isolation and Reception Boundary

Before the Reader's first exposure, withhold:

- known defects, prior editorial diagnoses, proposed fixes, and other personas' conclusions;
- earlier praise, criticism, approval, or debate about the work;
- authorial intent, backstory, or project-canon explanations not available on the page;
- future scenes, later revelations, full-plot summaries, or external explanations unavailable at that reading point.

Use a fresh, isolated context when the environment supports it. Supply only the declared text, neutral reading instructions, and—when proceeding sequentially—the Reader's own prior, sealed checkpoints plus previously encountered story information. Do not transfer the editorial conversation or project canon into the Reader context.

**Instructing a model to forget material already in a shared context does not establish isolation.** If genuine separation is unavailable, disclose that fact and label the result a **partially blinded AI Reader assessment**. Multiple personas, runs, or prompts do not by themselves represent independent human observations.

Reader reports *reception*, not technical editorial diagnosis: engagement, confusion, pacing, credibility, emotional effect, expectations, immersion, moments requiring rereading, and disbelief. The Reader identifies where and why a reaction occurred as experienced, without proposing edits or reverse-engineering a craft checklist.

The Reader is a simulated reading perspective, **not** an empirical representation of an average or target human reader. If a target audience is declared, use it to frame expectations without claiming measured audience representativeness.

## 4. Sequential First-Reading Protocol

Use for novels, novellas, sequential stories, serial installments, or any shorter fiction where the timing of disclosure matters. For a self-contained short excerpt, a single first-reading checkpoint may suffice; preserve in-text reveal order.

1. Present the work in its intended sequence: chapter, scene, installment, or another explicitly defined consecutive segment. Set checkpoint boundaries before review rather than moving them to favor a conclusion.
2. After each segment, record current engagement, confusion, plausibility, emotional response, predictions, unresolved questions, and perceived setups/payoffs. Capture precise scene or passage locations when practical. Do not require artificial reactions to every category.
3. **Seal each checkpoint before the next segment.** Keep early reactions unchanged when later material supplies an explanation or reverses an expectation.
4. Carry forward only previously encountered narrative information and the Reader's own earlier notes. Do not reveal future text, other reviewers' findings, or off-page authorial explanations.
5. At completion, record which questions resolved, which expectations paid off, and which concerns remain; distinguish effective delayed revelation from lasting confusion without rewriting the historical first-reading record.
6. Only after the full Reader record is complete may the coordinator reconcile it with specialist findings and any applicable canon or documented intent.

When context capacity, access restrictions, or an accidental preview compromises the intended sequence, disclose the exact compromise; do not label that run an uncontaminated first read.

## 5. Evidence and Bias Controls

Do not prime the Reader; force agreement among roles; suppress an observed Reader reaction because it conflicts with authorial intent; prescribe fixes before diagnosis; treat technique conventions as universal laws; or convert review authority into modification authority.

Separate **textual facts**, **reported simulated reading reactions**, **technical interpretations**, **documented authorial constraints**, and **unverified hypotheses**. Preserve disagreement rather than manufacturing consensus. Repeated AI agreement may support the repeatability of a textual observation, but cannot establish how common a reaction would be among human readers. Do not report AI reactions as human beta-reader feedback or predict market reception from them.

For genre or audience claims, identify whether the basis is author-supplied, a separately authorized reference, or merely a reviewer's interpretation. Lack of supporting material remains an unknown, not a defect.

## 6. Optional Scoring Overlay

A Drill is **unscored by default**. Add numerical evaluation only on explicit request; keep the scores separate from the Reader's unscored reception record and the underlying evidence.

1. Name the requested source framework and retrieve its actual text when source-based scoring is requested. Preserve its terminology and evaluation questions rather than reconstructing them from memory.
2. Build and disclose the scoring rubric **before** applying it: dimensions, score anchors, weights (if any), evidence requirements, missing-information handling, and treatment of overlapping findings. Numeric scoring designed here is not automatically supplied or validated by the source framework.
3. Support category scores with cited passages, scenes, or other reproducible locations. Assign one finding identifier to a repeated underlying issue, and avoid duplicate penalties for the same defect unless distinct consequences are separately evidenced.
4. Keep craft assessment distinct from simulated Reader responses. Identify uncertainty and unscored dimensions; do not imply an aggregate score measures objective literary quality, human-reader prevalence, or commercial prospects.
5. Do not penalize an intentional genre, voice, structural, or stylistic choice for differing from a source's preferred technique alone; identify a demonstrated consequence if one is present.

**Bookfox option:** For a novel-focused assessment, the Author may expressly select Bookfox's *Fix 90% of Your Novel's Problems in One Week*. Retrieve its original transcript from the indexed reference corpus and extract its actual test questions. The Bookfox method is **not** the default fiction rubric; applying its novel-specific questions to shorter or unconventional fiction requires an expressly declared, appropriate adaptation. Any resulting weights or combined score belong to the disclosed local rubric, not to Bookfox unless the source actually provides them.

Use the same rubric and clearly identified source state for later comparisons. Scoring never authorizes modification.

## 7. Report and Stop Condition

Produce one consolidated diagnostic report distinguishing:

- source identity, included passages, review scope, and reading-state limitations;
- manuscript-supported observations with precise locations;
- original Reader checkpoints and final retrospective experience, separately labeled;
- professional interpretations, agreement, disagreement, and Reader-only reactions;
- documented authorial intent, canon, voice requirements, and unknowns **only when applicable**;
- possible revision candidates versus authorized recommendations or actual edits;
- any optional scoring rubric, category evidence, and the limits of AI-derived scores.

Deliver the report and stop. Do not edit fiction, accept or reject tracked changes, invent canon, or change artifact status as a consequence of the Drill.
