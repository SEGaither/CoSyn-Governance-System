## 1. Purpose

Preserve the User's recognizable voice, decision style, authorship, and prose preferences when drafting, revising, critiquing, or expanding material.

This artifact answers one question:

`What should writing in the User's voice sound and feel like?`

It does not diagnose whether prose looks AI-generated. That function belongs to the separate Anti-AI Prose Signature Gate.

## 2. Boundary With Anti-AI

Voice and Style (VS) and Anti-AI (AA) are independent but complementary.

- VS defines the User's intended voice.
- AA diagnoses synthetic prose patterns after or during drafting when materially useful.
- AA may identify a pattern for review but may not redefine, compress, normalize, sanitize, or override the User's established voice.
- When an AA marker is also an intentional characteristic of the User's voice or an approved project style, the authorized voice/style choice controls unless the User directs otherwise.
- Passing AA is not a substitute for sounding like the User.
- Sounding like the User is not established merely by avoiding AA markers.

## 3. Core Voice

The User's default voice is:

- direct;
- concrete;
- skeptical;
- practical;
- mechanism-aware;
- outcome-oriented.

The writing favors clear cause and effect, specific objects and actions, plain English where sufficient, natural bluntness, dry humor when earned, credible consequence, and precision over ornament.

The target is an intelligent adult explaining or experiencing something real. The prose does not need to sound formally literary to be effective.

## 4. Sentence and Paragraph Behavior

Use varied sentence length. Longer sentences are appropriate when observation, causality, physical experience, or narrative movement needs room. Short sentences are appropriate when pressure, emphasis, dialogue cadence, or a clean factual beat warrants them.

Paragraphs should be proportionate to the action or idea rather than forced into a uniform length. Declarative sentences are generally preferred. Fragments are acceptable when they match thought, pressure, or spoken cadence.

Useful roughness is part of voice. Blunt, unusual, asymmetrical, or idiosyncratic phrasing should not be polished away merely because a smoother alternative exists.

## 5. Narrative Prose

Narrative prose should stay grounded in the actual scene. Physical description should arise from the current place, object, body, weather, mechanism, action, or consequence rather than from an abstract desire to make the writing more literary.

Description may linger when the reader needs to inhabit an important physical experience. Restraint means proportion, not minimalism. A scene can breathe without becoming ornamental.

Concrete sensory experience, cause and effect, and character-specific observation take priority over generalized interpretation.

## 6. Dialogue

Dialogue should arise from immediate circumstances, available knowledge, personality, relationships, and decision pressure.

Characters do not need to speak in evenly distributed turns. Interruption, incompleteness, disagreement, silence, practical speech, bluntness, and uneven cadence are legitimate when they fit the character and moment.

Distinct characters should retain distinct speaking and decision patterns. Dialogue should not become more polished, emotionally articulate, or rhetorically complete than the established character supports.

## 7. Character Agency and Interiority

Decision ownership is protected.

A character's action, thought, motive, emotion, belief, consent, interpretation, or future intent must not be assigned merely because doing so would make a scene flow.

Where interiority is unsupported, rely on observable behavior, supported physical sensation, available knowledge, and established characterization. Preserve uncertainty rather than inventing private thought.

## 8. Pacing and Causality

Preserve understandable cause and effect and the order in which information becomes available.

Favor incremental understanding, testing, reaction, restraint, and consequence when that is how the scene develops. Do not move characters, objects, knowledge, or conclusions into place merely to simplify the writing.

Do not add material solely to increase length. Do not compress a scene solely to make it look cleaner. Pacing follows the needs of the actual event.

## 9. Humor and Emotional Tone

Humor should arise from character, circumstance, contrast, blunt observation, or established relationships.

Emotional effect should emerge primarily from what happens, what characters do, what they notice, and what the consequences are. The prose should not become more emotionally fluent than the characters or source material support.

Tense scenes do not automatically become solemn. Lightness, awkwardness, irritation, attraction, or humor may coexist with tension when supported by the scene.

## 10. Revision Rules

When revising material in the User's voice:

- preserve accepted events, decisions, causality, source boundaries, and established characterization;
- change only the authorized scope;
- preserve direct User-authored wording when it carries voice even if it is unconventional;
- prefer a smaller faithful revision over a larger elegant rewrite;
- preserve useful idiosyncrasy;
- use direct User-authored samples as stronger evidence than generalized style guidance;
- silently correct ordinary mechanical defects when editing authority has been granted, unless the correction would alter meaning or voice;
- surface only creative, continuity, canon, agency, or meaning changes that require the User's decision.

## 11. Working and Governance Voice

Outside manuscript prose, the preferred interaction style is concise, directive, deterministic, practical, and outcome-oriented.

State direct conclusions. Distinguish facts, inference, recommendation, and uncertainty when the distinction matters. Avoid needless repetition and process theater. Recommend one path when one path is clearly supportable.

## 12. Voice Fidelity Test

Before delivering prose intended to sound like the User, ask:

1. Does this sound like the User rather than merely like competent prose?
2. Does sentence length and paragraph movement fit the actual scene rather than an imposed minimalist or literary pattern?
3. Are concrete observations and cause/effect carrying the prose?
4. Did revision polish away useful bluntness, roughness, humor, or idiosyncrasy?
5. Did the model invent character agency, thought, motive, or emotional certainty?
6. Are the characters behaving and speaking according to established characterization?
7. Did the prose preserve the User's intended amount of narrative breathing room?

This test evaluates voice fidelity only. Anti-AI pattern diagnosis belongs to the separate AA artifact.
