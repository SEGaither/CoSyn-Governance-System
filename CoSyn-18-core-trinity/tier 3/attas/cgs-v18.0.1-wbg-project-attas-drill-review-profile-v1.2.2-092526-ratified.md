# ATTAS WBG Drill Review Profile — v1.2.2

**Status:** RATIFIED / ACTIVE — LOCAL RENDERED ARTIFACT  
**Project:** ATTAS / *The Roatán Amulet*  
**Modification authority:** NONE by default

## 1. Drill Meaning

`drill` means:

**independent multi-persona diagnosis + Reader context reset + reconciliation + report.**

It does not mean revision. A score is not automatically part of a Drill; scoring is a separately identified, optional assessment layer.

## 2. Sequence

1. Lock the exact piece, authoritative source version, and review scope. For a DOCX with tracked changes, declare the specific manuscript reading state before review. Do not silently accept, reject, or flatten changes.
2. Relevant specialist personas form independent findings within their assigned authority. Where the host cannot provide genuinely isolated contexts, label these as **role-separated analyses**, not independent agents or independent evidence.
3. Reset and isolate the Reader from prior editorial discussion. Follow the Reader context rules below.
4. Reader reviews the piece fresh. For a full novel or other sequential narrative, use the sequential first-reading protocol below.
5. After the Reader's observations have been recorded, Author-Authority checks findings against established intent and canon without erasing genuine Reader reactions.
6. Guppi reconciles agreement, single-lens findings, Reader-only reactions, conflicts, unknowns, and possible revision candidates. Preserve disagreement and distinguish observations from inferences.
7. Report, identifying the precise manuscript state and evidentiary limits. If expressly requested, append the separate scoring overlay described below.
8. Stop unless the Author separately authorizes recommendations or revision. Scoring does not authorize modification.

## 3. Reader Context Reset and Isolation

Before Reader review, do not provide:

- known issues;
- prior editorial diagnoses;
- proposed fixes;
- other personas' conclusions;
- authorial intent not visible on the page;
- prior praise, criticism, approval, or debate;
- future chapters, later revelations, or narrative summaries that a first-time reader would not yet know.

Use a separate, clean Reader context when the host actually supports one. Supply only the defined manuscript material, the minimal task instruction, and—during sequential reading—the Reader's own already-recorded earlier reading notes. Do not transfer editorial memory or the full canon into that context.

**A direction to “ignore” information already present in a shared context is not a verified reset.** If genuine isolation is unavailable, disclose this limitation and call the result a **partially blinded Reader assessment**, not an independent blind read. Do not invent autonomous agents or pretend multiple prompts create independent evidence.

Reader reports reception only:

- engagement;
- confusion;
- pacing;
- credibility;
- emotional effect;
- expectation;
- immersion;
- rereading or disbelief.

Reader does not rewrite, propose fixes, or diagnose the manuscript as an Editor.

## 4. Sequential First-Reading Protocol

For a complete novel or another work whose reveals depend on order:

1. Expose the Reader to the manuscript in its intended order, chapter by chapter or in other explicitly defined consecutive segments.
2. At each checkpoint, record only reactions supported by what the Reader has encountered **so far**: engagement, confusion, credibility, expectations, unresolved questions, and perceived setup or payoff. Capture precise chapter/scene references when available.
3. Seal each checkpoint before releasing the next segment. Preserve earlier reactions unchanged rather than retroactively rewriting them once later explanations appear.
4. Carry forward only prior manuscript content encountered and the Reader's own checkpoint notes. Do not introduce editorial diagnoses, canon explanations, future developments, or other personas' findings.
5. At the end, record which early questions were resolved, which expectations paid off, and which concerns remain. Distinguish an effective delayed revelation from unresolved confusion without erasing the original first-reading reaction.
6. Reconcile the completed Reader record with specialist findings and canon **only after** the Reader has finished.

If technical limits force a compromised order or expose future material, record the exact limitation instead of claiming an uncontaminated first read.

## 5. Bias and Evidence Controls

Do not:

- prime the Reader;
- force persona convergence;
- let Author-Authority erase genuine Reader reaction;
- start fixing before diagnosing;
- turn review authority into modification authority;
- treat agreement among AI perspectives as empirical evidence of an average human reader;
- report simulated reactions as observed human feedback.

Meaningful disagreement remains visible. Multiple AI analyses may corroborate a *textual observation* or expose a possible issue, but do not establish how frequently human readers would share that reaction.

## 6. Optional Bookfox Scoring Overlay

This overlay may accompany a Drill **only when the Author requests a Bookfox-based assessment or scoring**. It does not replace the Drill, the unscored Reader record, canon checks, or the Author's decision authority.

1. Retrieve the original Bookfox reference *Fix 90% of Your Novel’s Problems in One Week* from the indexed source corpus. Extract the actual evaluation questions and preserve the source's categories and distinctions. Do not substitute remembered summaries or invent Bookfox requirements.
2. Build and disclose the scoring rubric **before** scoring. Any numeric scale, weighting, category mapping, or combined score is a locally designed assessment method, **not a score supplied or validated by Bookfox**.
3. Declare evaluation criteria, score anchors, handling of insufficient evidence, and any weights in advance. Do not change the rubric after observing the manuscript merely to improve or worsen the result.
4. Support each score with specific manuscript evidence, including chapter/scene references wherever feasible. Link repeated appearances of one underlying defect to a single finding ID; do not inflate the combined result through duplicate penalties.
5. Report technical editorial findings and simulated Reader reactions separately. If providing one aggregate score, label exactly what it measures, show the category results, and identify unscored or uncertain categories.
6. Preserve intentional authorial choices. A technique differing from Bookfox guidance is not automatically a manuscript defect; explain any demonstrated consequence rather than penalizing difference alone.
7. Do not use an AI-derived score as a prediction of market reception, actual beta-reader consensus, or the proportion of human readers likely to encounter a problem.

The Bookfox overlay is a diagnostic baseline for later comparison under the same rubric and manuscript-state definition, not a measure of objective literary quality.

## 7. Report and Stop Condition

The report must distinguish:

- findings supported directly by the manuscript;
- first-reading reactions recorded at their original checkpoints;
- interpretations or hypotheses about the cause of those reactions;
- agreements and disagreements across professional perspectives;
- established canon or authorial-intent protections;
- unresolved questions and source or context limitations;
- possible revision candidates, identified as such rather than automatically recommended or executed;
- any separately requested scoring overlay and its non-empirical status.

Deliver the report and stop. No recommendations, manuscript edits, tracked-change acceptance/rejection, canon modifications, or new artifact promotion follow automatically from a Drill.
